## 🎯 Project Summary

**Abstract**
Human papillomavirus (HPV) is the primary etiological agent of cervical cancer. However, progression to carcinoma is slow and occurs in only a fraction of infected women. Although viral oncoproteins interfere with central mechanisms involved in the DNA damage response and repair, it remains poorly understood how HPV transcriptional activity relates to epithelial heterogeneity and the modulation of these pathways during tumor progression. This project aims to investigate, at single-cell resolution, the association between HPV transcriptional activity, cellular heterogeneity in cervical cancer, and the definition of epithelial states with differential activation of DNA damage and repair pathways, as well as to explore the crosstalk between these cellular states and the tumor immune microenvironment. To this end, we previously developed a metatranscriptomic approach applied to bulk RNA-seq data, capable of detecting viral transcripts and assessing their association with host biological processes. However, this strategy does not allow the identification of specific cell types and cellular states present within the tumor tissue. To overcome this limitation, we propose to integrate this approach with single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data, enabling the investigation of viral activity across different epithelial populations within the tumor. Preliminary analyses integrating bulk RNA-seq and scRNA-seq data demonstrate the feasibility of the proposed approach and its ability to identify HPV transcripts in transcriptomic datasets. Applying this methodology to an expanded set of samples will enable integrative analyses, including the inference of biological pathways and regulatory networks associated with HPV transcriptional activity in epithelial cells.
